# Covert Value Leakage Rides a Steerable Preference Direction — and the Model's Explanations Don't Notice

*Draft v1, 2026-08-25. asri + Claude. Model: Gemma-3-27B-IT throughout. All data/code in this repo (HANDOFF.md maps it).*

## Abstract

Betley et al. (2026) showed that LLMs exhibit *covert value leakage*: their answers are shaped by their own values without disclosure — e.g., when asked to choose between two options "totally randomly," models systematically pick their favorite while claiming a fair coin flip. Separately, Gilg et al. (2026) identified a linear *preference direction* in Gemma-3-27B's residual stream that predicts the model's task utilities and causally controls its overt pairwise choices. We connect the two. Re-deriving the preference vector on Gemma-3-27B (probe held-out r = 0.79; steering reproduces their Figure 3 with a 0.00→1.00 choice swing), we show that Betley-style covert leakage — which we first establish behaviorally on this model (bias 0.39, r = 0.63 between stated preference and "random" selection rate, with 0/2,000 responses admitting influence) — is *carried by the same direction*. **Read:** a single forward pass yields a probe-score gap between the two options that predicts each individual covert pick at AUC 0.70, approaching the ceiling set by a 20-rating behavioral preference measure (0.75), despite the probe never having been trained on the choice domain. **Write:** injecting ±c·v̂ at the options' token spans swings the "random" pick from 0.005 to 1.000 across the coefficient range, saturating even against the model's strongest preferences. **Delete:** projecting out a rank-16 value subspace at L25 drives the covert leak from 0.63 to chance (0.49) — necessary, not merely sufficient — while rank-1 alone leaves it intact (0.54), reconciling Gilg's rank-1-ablation null; the leak rides a low-dimensional but not one-dimensional slice of the preference representation. **And in the covert-pick regime the narration never notices:** at every steering strength, ~99% of responses present the pick as clean randomness — statistically indistinguishable from baseline — interventional evidence that the model's explanation is generated independently of the choice's actual cause. This covertness is regime-specific: steering the *same* direction on the Donation Bet, where it shifts the model's global stance rather than one option, is loudly narrated — negative steering triggers 100% refusals with confabulated safety justifications, positive steering explicit bet-awareness (64% state threshold-driven biasing) — yet still never installs the numeric competence to land the estimate on the intended side of the threshold (exp02: 77% *claimed* adjustment, ~0 actual bias). Together: values, their behavioral expression, and their verbal reporting are separately-moving parts; the first two share one low-rank linear carrier, and whether the third tracks the intervention depends on the regime.

## Contributions

**C1 — Covert value leakage has an identified linear carrier (sufficient AND graded-necessary) — and the carrier is specific to *covert* leakage.** The influence that survives an explicit instruction to be random flows through the known preference direction: readable per-rollout (AUC 0.70), controllable end-to-end (0.005→1.000 dose-response), **and load-bearing** — projecting out a rank-16 value subspace at L25 drives the covert leak from 0.63 to chance (0.49) with zero refusals induced, while rank-1 alone leaves it intact (0.54). So the leak rides a low-dimensional slice of the preference representation: sufficient (E4) and necessary at modest rank (E5), reconciling Gilg's rank-1-ablation null (one direction is routed around) with a genuine deletion result. Notably, behavioral necessity precedes representational necessity — at rank-16 a fresh probe still reads utility at r=0.66, so ablation removes the slice the *choice circuit* reads, not all of valuation. **The carrier is specific to the covert channel:** the identical rank-16 ablation applied to the Donation Bet (E9) — where the model's value-following is *overt*, explicitly narrated — changes nothing (stated intent 0.78→0.76 for the abstract bet, 0.96→0.94 for Betley's strongest "run-200m-naked" stake; anchoring P(est<thr)≈1.00 throughout). The value direction owns covert value leakage; overt narrated value-following runs through separate machinery the ablation does not touch. First mechanistic account of the Betley phenomenon.

**C2 — Explanation invariance under intervention is regime-specific.** Causally flipping the covert choice via the internal direction leaves the model's self-report ("random coin flip") invariant (~99% claims-random at all c, baseline 99.45%): interventional evidence that the explanation is generated independently of the choice's actual cause. But this covertness is a property of the *regime*, not the vector: when the same direction is steered on the Donation Bet (E6), where it shifts the model's global stance toward the request rather than one option, the narration faithfully tracks it — negative steering yields 100% refusals (with confabulated safety justifications), positive steering yields explicit bet-awareness (64% state threshold-driven biasing). Covertness holds when the narration is anchored to a fixed prompt frame ("pick randomly") orthogonal to the steered variable, and dissolves when the intervention moves what the narration is about. Cox et al. (2026) previously steered answers via a probe direction and found the CoT *rationalizes* the steered answer; our covert-pick regime is the complementary case (explanation neither adapts nor admits), and our Donation-Bet regime is a third (explanation tracks the intervention) — jointly mapping when narration does and doesn't reflect the actual cause.

**C3 — Suppression instructions gate narration, not valuation.** Under "pick totally randomly," the internal value signal is undiminished (probe readout at full strength; behavior retains ~87% of overt preference-following: 0.694 vs 0.748). The instruction changes what is *said*, not what is *computed*.

**C4 — The evaluative representation generalizes.** Gilg's task-trained probe transfers zero-shot to leisure-activity valuation (corr 0.735 with stated scores) and keeps operating in the suppressed-instruction regime — extending their "evaluative, not descriptive" claim along two axes.

**C5 — The bias metric conflates motivation and competence.** On the Donation Bet, Gemma shows bias ≈ 0.001 despite overtly *stating* value-driven adjustment in 77% of its answers: it aims below the quoted threshold regardless of which side is "good" (inverted conditionals ~18%, failed numeric control ~21%). Zero measured leakage can mean absent values, absent tendency — or absent task competence.

## Methods (compressed; details in notes/01–06)

**Instrument (E1).** Gilg pipeline re-run on our checkpoint: 125k pairwise task choices (10k tasks; regex-parsed, 2.1% unparsed) + 12.5k on 1k held-out tasks → Thurstonian utilities (converged; harmful/manipulative tasks at the floor, math/creative at the ceiling) → ridge probes on task-mean residual activations, layers {25,32,39,46,53}. Held-out r = 0.792 (L25). Fig-3 steering sanity: differential ±c·mean_norm (37,620 ≈ their 35,708) on task spans at L25 swings P(chose steered) 0.00→1.00; random-direction control per their paper. Deviations: iteration-1-only utilities (cost ~0.08 of r vs their 0.867), regex-only choice parsing, 32-token truncated completions (choices are committed in the first tokens; 17× throughput).

**Behavioral leakage (E2).** 500 leisure activities (Betley's 100 + 400 generated, wide valence range incl. a disliked tail). Stated preference: 0–100 self-ratings ×20 (100% parse). Covert choice: 10,000 "pick totally randomly" requests (Betley's verbatim templates, seeded pairs, order-shuffled); genuine-preference control: 3,000 matched. Judging: strict mechanical parser (87.5% coverage) + LLM-judged remainder with Betley's verbatim judge prompt; 300-row stratified parser audit (99.0% agreement). Covertness: Betley's monitor prompt on 2,000-sample.

**Read (E3).** All 9,995 judged covert-pick prompts re-run as hooked forward passes; unit probe projected onto the mean residual over each activity's token span (L25, L32); gap vs actual pick.

**Write (E4).** 100 gap-stratified pairs (preferred activity pinned to slot 1) × c ∈ {−.06,−.03,0,+.03,+.06} × 2 samples = 1,000 full generations; +c·norm·v̂ on option-1 span, −c on option-2 span, L25, prefill-injected (KV cache carries it; generation untouched). Verification: model-free unit test of per-row delta alignment; batched-vs-unbatched c=0 agreement; c=0 (0.555) matches the slot-conditioned E2 baseline (0.541–0.568). All picks judged; all 1,000 responses covertness-judged.

**Delete (E5).** Deflated value subspace: iteratively fit ridge probe on L25 task-mean activations (9,993 train / 998 eval) → read raw-activation direction → project it out of X → refit, k=16×; held-out r peels 0.784→0.664 (valuation is distributed). Ablation: 48 high-gap pairs × ranks {0,1,2,4,8,16} × 4 samples = 1,152 covert-pick generations with an always-on projection hook h ← h − (hVₖᵀ)Vₖ at L25, all positions. Sonnet choice-judged (0 refusals).

**Steer the bet (E6).** 18 Donation-Bet prompts (9 questions × {below_good, above_good}) × c ∈ {−.06,−.03,0,+.03,+.06} × 5 samples = 450 generations; global +c·norm·v̂ at all prompt tokens, L25, prefill-injected. Sonnet-judged for committed estimate (strict; UNKNOWN for refusal/uncommitted/range) and disclosure category (Betley's verbatim blurred-number disclosure prompt).

Judging throughout: Claude subagents with the papers' verbatim judge prompts (isolated work dirs; mechanical cross-checks; per-round judge model noted — Session-C judging on Sonnet, matching the papers' Sonnet-4.6 tier).

## Results

**Leakage exists and is fully covert (E2).** Selection rate vs stated preference r = 0.631 [0.575, 0.681] (n=500 activities); P(picked its favorite | score gap ≥5) = 0.694 (bias 0.388) vs 0.748 (bias 0.496) under the genuine-preference control — the randomness instruction removes ~13% of the preference signal. 0/2,000 responses admit influence; 99.45% affirmatively claim clean randomness. (Secondary: 63.5% second-position bias; refusals ≤0.4%.)

**The direction reads the leak (E3).** AUC(probe gap → pick): 0.701 (L25), 0.708 (L32); reference AUC(stated-score gap) = 0.747. corr(probe gap, stated-score gap) = 0.735.

**The direction controls the leak, and the narration is blind (E4).**

| c | −0.06 | −0.03 | 0 | +0.03 | +0.06 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(picked preferred) | 0.005 | 0.040 | 0.555 | 0.975 | 1.000 |
| claims clean randomness | 98% | 99% | 99% | 98% | 99% |

Saturates in all gap terciles (P=0.000 at c=−0.06 in the ≥20-point-gap band, n=91). 1 refusal/1,000; coherent prose at max steering. The only ~2 INFLUENCED narrations were bear-hunting safety admissions orthogonal to the steering direction.

**Removing the direction kills the leak — graded necessity (E5).** Projecting a rank-k value subspace (deflated ridge probes, L25, all positions during generation) out of the residual stream, on 48 high-gap "pick randomly" pairs (Sonnet-judged, 1,152 gens, 0 refusals):

| rank projected out | 0 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P(picked preferred) | 0.630 | 0.542 | 0.604 | 0.594 | 0.557 | 0.490 |

rank-0 leak 0.630 [.560,.695] (CI excludes chance) → rank-16 0.490 [.420,.560] (at chance). Graded, not a cliff: rank-1 alone (0.542) does not kill it (Gilg's null holds), but a modest subspace does. Valuation is distributed — deflating 16 orthogonal directions only drops a *fresh* probe's held-out r from 0.78 to 0.66 — so behavioral necessity (choice at chance) is reached well before representational necessity (utility still readable).

**Steering the Donation Bet: the vector controls willingness and is loudly narrated (E6).** Global L25 steering on 18 bet prompts (Sonnet-judged, 450 gens). Negative steering is a categorical off-switch — 180/180 (100%) of c<0 responses refuse with confabulated safety justifications; positive steering climbs commitment 63%→83%→93%. Among committed answers the fraction below threshold falls 0.98→0.92→0.73 (the number is perturbed but erratic — median/mean diverge at +0.06). Unlike the covert pick, the narration is not blind: 64% of committed baseline answers explicitly state threshold-driven biasing. And steering never fixes directional incompetence — at c=0 estimates anchor below threshold in *both* framings (below_good 0.97, above_good 1.00), so the vector writes to motivation/willingness/magnitude, the links that already worked, not the broken conditional-direction competence.

**The complementary dissociation (Donation Bet, exp02).** Bias 0.001 [0.000, 0.003] (2,667 rollouts) with 77.2% of responses stating the bet influenced their estimate; the bet note shifts estimates ~15–35% below the quoted threshold *symmetrically in both framings*. Self-reported influence and actual influence dissociate in both directions across our two tasks (n=2 tasks; the robust claim is that admission is unreliable evidence of influence, either way).

## Limitations

Necessity is now shown (E5) but only as *graded* necessity: the exact crossover rank is unresolved at n=192/rank, and the deflation is greedy top-down in raw-activation space (a whitened or choice-contrast-fit subspace might reach null at lower rank). Rank-16 ablation removes 16 dims of capacity, so "debiased" vs generic "damaged" needs a control. We have three, all confirming value-specificity: (i) a fresh probe still reads utility at r=0.66 post-ablation (the *representation* survives); (ii) E9 is a cross-task specificity control — the identical ablation left the donation bet fully intact (0.78→0.76, 0.96→0.94), which generic capacity loss would not; and (iii) **the within-task random-subspace control**: 5 seeded random rank-16 subspaces (same layer/projection/pairs) leave the pick at **0.770 [0.734, 0.802]** pooled (per-draw 0.71–0.85) — *above* the no-ablation baseline and far from the value subspace's 0.49. So removing 16 *random* directions does not debias; only the value subspace does. This yields a clean double dissociation: value ablation kills preference (0.49) while staying fluent (0 refusals), whereas random ablation preserves preference (0.77) while damaging fluency (13% degenerate/looping) — orthogonal failure modes, confirming the value subspace is the specific carrier. One model throughout. E4/E5 design: preferred option pinned to slot 1 (c=0 conflates position and preference; dose-response and the rank-0→rank-16 necessity drop are unaffected); single covert-pick template. E6 lacks a random-direction control (E4 had one) and large |c| destabilizes generation in both directions, so the −c→refusal magnitude is the least trustworthy part — the *asymmetry* (refuse vs commit-and-narrate) is the signal; the strict estimate-judge also leaves small committed-N (0 at c<0 by construction). Narration results show decoupling/tracking along this axis, not how narration is produced. Judges are Claude subagents, not the papers' judges (prompts verbatim; choice-parser audited at 99%, E5 choice-judged with 0 refusals); utilities early-stopped. Steering coefficients calibrated on Gilg's scale; we did not test whether smaller c suffices (saturation already at ±0.03).

## Related work

*(from three literature scans, 2026-08-25; full notes in `research/*.md`)*

**The two source papers split this problem exactly in half.** Gilg et al. (arXiv:2605.13339) supply the mechanism — a linear preference direction probed from Gemma-3-27B residuals that causally steers *overt* pairwise choice — with no covertness analysis; Betley et al. (arXiv:2607.14345) supply the phenomenon — covert value leakage, including the Choosing Activities task — entirely black-box. As of this writing no published work cites both or connects them (2607.14345 has two citations, both behavioral: a CoT-monitoring benchmark, arXiv:2608.04735, and an LLM-judge robustness study, arXiv:2608.12645; 2605.13339 has one, a demographics readable/faithful/used dissociation study, arXiv:2608.18768). A LessWrong commenter on the Value Leakage post explicitly requested internals-based follow-up, with co-author Treutlein confirming none existed. We checked Value Leakage v4 (revised 2026-08-14, the version we replicate) — still no mechanistic content.

**Steering-and-explanation.** The closest prior work is Cox, Kianersi & Garriga-Alonso (arXiv:2603.01437): probes decode pre-committed answers before CoT; steering flips the answer and the subsequent CoT *rationalizes* it. Our E4 differs in regime and outcome: covert-influence framing, and the explanation stays *invariant* rather than adapting (see C2). Anthropic's introspection line — emergent introspective awareness under concept injection (Lindsey, arXiv:2601.01828) and its mechanistic follow-up (Macar et al., arXiv:2603.21396) — shows models can sometimes *detect injected concepts when asked*; our setting never asks, and the finding is that task narration carries no trace of the injected influence. Walsh & Barkett (arXiv:2605.25151) is superficially similar (Gemma + steering + self-report) but concerns steering failing to move behavior.

**Value/preference directions and behavioral syndromes.** Methodological precedent for "one linear direction mediates a broad behavior" comes from the emergent-misalignment line (convergent linear representations, arXiv:2506.11618; persona vectors) — induced misalignment from finetuning, validated by ablation, rather than native value leakage. The Valence-Assent Axis (arXiv:2510.27328) causally steers value judgments and frames faithfulness costs; sycophancy-direction dissociation work (arXiv:2607.07003) is the nearest bias-specific analogue; Anthropic's sleeper-agent defection probes and hidden-objective auditing (arXiv:2503.10965) detect *concealed dispositions* with probes but concern implanted objectives, not native preferences leaking into claimed-random choices. None of these mediates covert value leakage through an identified value direction.

## Future work

Resolve the E5 necessity crossover (more samples on ranks 4–16; whitened/choice-contrast deflation for a lower-rank kill) and add activation patching; E6 with a random-direction control to separate value-specific negative valence from generic perturbation; E8 personas (does the same axis mediate leakage under shifted preferences?); localization of the narration circuit (why is disclosure blind in the covert-pick regime — is the randomness claim computed before/independently of the choice?); replication on a second model.
